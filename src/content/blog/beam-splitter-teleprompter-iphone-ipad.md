A beam-splitter is the teleprompter setup that TV uses: a piece of semi-transparent mirror glass angled at 45° in front of the lens, with the script reflected onto it. You read the text *on the glass* while looking straight into the camera behind it. The eye contact is perfect - not "close to the lens," but literally through it.

The modern version costs a fraction of broadcast gear: a rig from Neewer, Glide Gear, or Desview, your camera behind the glass, and an iPhone or iPad as the display. The hardware is the easy half. The app choice is where setups succeed or die, and it comes down to two features. (Disclosure: I build VoicePrompter, which I'll recommend for both - here's why, checkably.)

## Feature one: mirroring

You're reading a reflection, so the app has to flip the text horizontally or every word renders backward in the glass. This sounds trivial but plenty of apps bury it or lack it. In [VoicePrompter](https://voiceprompter.app/ipad/) it's the one-tap **Mirroring** mode - flip it, drop the device in the tray, done.

## Feature two: nobody can touch the screen

Here's what beam-splitter guides rarely mention: once the device is in the rig, under the glass hood, **you physically can't touch it mid-take**. That's exactly why broadcast prompters always came with an operator, and why consumer rigs push Bluetooth remotes - a clicker in your hand while you present, one more thing to fumble.

Voice tracking dissolves the whole problem. The script follows your actual words - pause and it waits, ad-lib and it catches you on return, restart an earlier sentence and it scrolls back to meet you. No operator, no remote in your palm, no walking around the camera between takes. In my testing, whole-script tracking (rather than fixed speed or forward-only following) is the difference between a rig you use once and a rig you use every week - the full app-by-app breakdown is in [my iPhone comparison](/blog/ios-teleprompter-apps-compared/) and [the iPad ranking](/blog/best-teleprompter-app-for-ipad/).

## The five-minute setup

1. **Mount**: camera behind the glass, iPhone/iPad in the tray below it. Level the rig at eye height.
2. **Load the script**: in VoicePrompter, copy it from anywhere and start from the clipboard in one tap.
3. **Flip**: enable Mirroring. Text now reads correctly in the glass.
4. **Scale**: push the font size until it's comfortable from your speaking distance; set the active-line position near the lens center.
5. **Talk**: voice tracking runs on-device (60+ languages, offline). If you need to jump around the script hands-free, voice commands ("go next", "go back", "go to start") work without touching anything.

One honest caveat: glass rigs cut a little light to the camera and add reflections to manage - budget ten minutes for angles the first time. After that, it's the most natural-looking prompting setup that exists, and the one place where an iPad earns its keep most: bigger glass, bigger text, easier reading.
